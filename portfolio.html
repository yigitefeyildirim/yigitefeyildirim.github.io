Kensai disaster-response platform

Senior design project · ML, routing, hardware

  • 5 damage classes per pixel
  • 256×256 patches
  • 8-connected A*

A portable, off-grid platform for post-earthquake rescue: a LoRa and Bluetooth sensor transmitter, a receiver, an offline-first phone app and a dashboard for rescuers.

I built the damage-assessment backend. A U-Net with a ResNet-34 backbone, trained on the xBD dataset, labels every pixel of an aerial image with one of five damage levels. The damage map becomes a cost grid with buffers around damaged buildings, and an A* planner routes people to an official assembly area, then to the nearest verified open area. It flags helicopter extraction only when no ground route exists, and keeps working on cached results when the network is down. I also contributed to the hardware design: microcontroller, GNSS and LoRa options.

Predictive maintenance on factory conveyors

Mercedes-Benz Türk · LSTM, PLC data

  • 6 months of data, 1 s sampling
  • 5 s prediction horizon
  • 2 conveyor types, 2 models

Motor current (and, on the second conveyor type, torque) had been logged every second for six months. I pulled the data straight from the PLCs with Snap7 and trained two LSTM models, one per conveyor type, that predict within the next five seconds whether the current will peak and the machine will raise an error.

The goal was simple: give maintenance teams a warning before a stoppage instead of after it.

Wideband amplifier with automatic gain control

Analog design · LTspice, Proteus, hand-soldered PCB

  • 200 kHz to 2 MHz
  • 50 Ω source, 470 Ω load
  • 15 V supply, under 100 mA

An amplifier that holds its output near 1 V peak while the input swings from 10 mV to 100 mV, with an AGC loop closing the gain. I calculated the component values and simulated the design in LTspice, where the output stayed between 0.90 and 1.05 V at both ends of the band.

I then designed the single-layer PCB with Proteus, fabricated the board and soldered every component myself.

Algorithmic trading as a Markov decision process

Reinforcement learning and dynamic programming

  • 35,343 states, γ = 0.95
  • V* = 40.70, matched within 1%
  • 5 solution methods

I derived the stationary distributions of the market and price processes analytically, built a simulator and checked it against the theory (maximum error 0.0012 over 475,000 samples). Value iteration and policy iteration solve the MDP exactly. Q-learning recovers over 80% of the optimal return from interaction alone.

REINFORCE with a baseline suffered from variance. A one-step Actor–Critic nearly doubled its return and cut the bankruptcy rate from 28% to 16%. A policy learned from a model estimated on only 2,000 transitions was indistinguishable from the optimum.

Student performance prediction, from scratch

Statistical learning · no ML libraries

  • 20 features, 5-fold CV
  • Ridge R² 0.66, MAE 1.40

I implemented ridge regression, a multilayer perceptron (SGD, backpropagation) and a random forest entirely by hand, without any machine learning library, and compared them under one cross-validation setup. Rare high exam scores were handled with oversampling and a weighted loss. Ridge regression won; the neural network and random forest landed near R² 0.47.

Lighting-aware reference image editing

Diffusion models · PyTorch

  • Training-free second pass
  • LAB luminance, σ = 60 blur

Inserted objects such as sunglasses often look pasted onto a portrait because their shadows follow the reference photo instead of the scene. I extended an IP-Adapter and LEdits++ pipeline with a second diffusion pass that reads the portrait's lighting (a blurred luminance field turned into a shadow map) and modulates the adapter, using a custom hybrid attention processor with core, ring and inclusion masks. Identity is preserved while the object adopts the scene's illumination.

Signature verification on CEDAR

Computer vision · classical and deep models

  • 2,640 images, 55 signers
  • About 83% test accuracy (k-NN, HOG)

We compared SVM, random forest, k-NN, XGBoost, logistic regression, an MLP and a ViT on genuine-versus-forged signatures, with HOG and LBP features and a writer-independent split to test generalization to unseen signers. About half of the models were implemented from scratch, the rest came from scikit-learn. k-NN with HOG performed best and HOG clearly beat LBP.
